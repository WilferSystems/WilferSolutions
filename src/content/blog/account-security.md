title: "Your digital security matters: protect your accounts"

Protecting your accounts does not require technical expertise. A few simple choices can help safeguard your email, social accounts and work tools. The goal is not to worry, but to build practical habits.

## Turn on two-factor authentication

Alongside your password, this method asks for a second confirmation when you sign in. It may be a temporary code in an authentication app or a security key. Start with your main email and the accounts that matter most.

## Use unique, strong passwords

If you reuse a password and one service has a problem, other accounts using the same password may also be at risk. Choose a long, different password for every service. A memorable phrase that is hard to guess can work well.

## Consider a password manager

A reputable manager stores unique passwords and helps create new ones. Protect the manager account with a strong master password and, if available, two-factor authentication. Download apps only from official sites or stores.

## Keep systems and apps up to date

Updates often include security fixes as well as improvements. Automatic updates for your operating system, browser and important apps can reduce the task list.

## Review signed-in sessions and devices

Every so often, check the devices or sessions connected to your main accounts. Sign out of ones you no longer use or do not recognize. If something looks unusual, change your password and review recovery options.

## Be cautious with unexpected links

Before opening an urgent link, check who sent it and where it leads. If an organization asks you to act quickly, open its app or type its known address directly into your browser.

## Never share verification codes

Sign-in codes are personal, even when someone asking for one claims to be support or someone you trust. Do not send codes to another person by chat or phone.

## Keep backups

A copy of important files on a separate device or trusted service can help after hardware failure or accidental deletion. Check from time to time that you can restore a file.

## Set safe recovery methods

Keep your recovery email and phone number current, and store backup codes privately. This gives you another option if you lose access to your usual device.

## One step at a time

You do not need to change everything today. Start by protecting your main email with a unique password and two-factor authentication; review other accounts at your own pace.
